## Problem Statement

There are approximately $2.85 \times 10^{22}$ copper atoms in a 3.00 g sample. What is the total charge of all of the electrons in the sample?

> [!question]- I Need a Hint!
> Each copper atom has 29 electrons.

> [!example]- Solution
> Each copper atom has 29 electrons, and the charge on a single electron is $1.602 \times 10^{-19} \: C$. Thus, the total charge of the electrons in the copper sample is:
>
> $$
> Q = \left ( 2.85 \times 10^{22} \: atoms \right ) \left ( \frac{29 \: e^-}{1 \: atom} \right ) \left ( \frac{1.602 \times 10^{-19} \: C}{1 \: e^-} \right )
> $$
>
> $$
> Q = 1.32 \times 10^5 \: C
> $$
