## Problem Statement

Astronomical distances are sometimes described in terms of light years. As you might guess, a light year is the distance that light in a vacuum will travel in one year. How many meters is one light year? The speed of light in a vacuum is $c = 299,792,458 \: m/s$.

## Solution

We can solve this problem using dimensional analysis. Let's start by calculating the number of seconds in a year:

$$
(1 \: year) \left ( \frac{365.25 \: days}{1 \: year} \right ) \left ( \frac{24 \: hours}{1 \: day} \right ) \left ( \frac{60 \: minutes}{1 \: hour} \right ) \left ( \frac{60 \: seconds}{1 \: minute} \right )= 31,557,600 \: seconds
$$

Now we can multiply by the speed of light to get the final answer:

$$
\left ( 299,792,458 \: m/s \right ) \left ( 31,557,600 \: s \right )= 9,460,730,472,580,800 \: m \approx 9.46 \times 10^{15} \: m
$$
